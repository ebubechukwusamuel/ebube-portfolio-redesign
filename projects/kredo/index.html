Product Design

Kredo

Kredo is a freelancer management product built around the journey from a new client request to delivered work and payment follow-up. I designed the experience to keep client, project and billing context connected, so freelancers can find the next action without jumping between separate tools. The screen set covers the main workspace, requests, clients, projects, proposals, invoices, time tracking, expenses, onboarding, settings and client-facing invoice states.

Open KredoView Kredo screens in FigmaView source code

Problem space

Freelancers often manage client details, project work, proposals, time, expenses and payment reminders in different places. I approached this as a workflow problem: how might one workspace make the status of work and money owed easier to see, from the first request through to invoice follow-up?

Design approach

I grouped the main workspace around the tasks freelancers repeat: requests, clients, projects, proposals, invoices, time and expenses. The dashboard brings client and project counts, proposal volume, revenue and pending invoice amounts into one scan, with quick actions and an active timer nearby. Invoice details keep line items, tax, due date, payment history and remaining balance together. Client-facing pages separate the handoff from the internal workspace.

Key design decisions

  • Organize the workspace around the client-to-payment workflow instead of making users manage separate mini tools.
  • Show client, project, proposal and invoice status together so work and payment follow-up can be understood in one scan.
  • Keep invoice totals, due dates, payment history and outstanding balance in the same detail view.
  • Surface quick actions and the active timer on the dashboard to support everyday work, not just reporting.
  • Separate public client-facing invoice states from internal management screens.

Product screens

The dashboard, project list and invoice detail are exports from the supplied Figma file. Financial amounts and client records are illustrative, not live revenue or real client data.

What I would validate next

I would test the end-to-end journey with freelancers, from receiving a request to sending a proposal, recording work, raising an invoice and confirming payment. I would also test partial payments, overdue invoices, interrupted timers, expense corrections and duplicate submissions. I would measure task completion and error rates only after observing real users; the screens alone do not prove time saved or payment performance.

What I chose not to ship yet

The screens show the product UI and its intended workflow. Client names, project counts, revenue figures, dates and payment rows are sample interface content. Paystack and Stripe payment flows, webhooks, authentication and permission handling need end-to-end verification before the portfolio claims they work reliably in production.
