Product Design

FlowTrack

FlowTrack is a project and task management product designed around shared visibility of work. Teams can view projects, inspect task status and ownership, see personal workload in a member dashboard, manage team membership and communicate through activity, submissions and chat. The case study focuses on the interface and intended workflow, rather than claiming measured productivity outcomes.

Open FlowTrackView FlowTrack screens in FigmaView source code

Problem space

When task owners, deadlines, submissions and team conversations are spread across separate tools, it becomes harder to see what is moving and what needs attention. I focused on making project scope, task status and personal priorities visible without removing the context needed to act on them.

Design approach

I used a consistent workspace navigation to separate projects and tasks from team and communication areas. Project detail keeps task title, assignee, due date, status, submission and actions in a single table. The member dashboard adds an overdue alert, assigned-task counts, a task-state chart and upcoming deadlines. The team screen gives membership a dedicated place, while chat, activity and submissions support work around the task itself.

Key design decisions

  • Keep task title, assignee, due date, status, submission and actions together so people can assess a task without opening every record.
  • Use an overdue alert and task summary on the member dashboard to point attention toward work that needs action.
  • Give team membership its own space instead of mixing access management into task editing.
  • Keep submissions, activity and chat close to project work to support handoffs and follow-up.
  • Show task states and deadlines explicitly, and validate permission, empty and error states before relying on the workflow.

Product screens

The project detail, member dashboard and team screens are taken from the supplied Figma file. The project detail view is a task table with status and ownership fields, not a Kanban board.

What I would validate next

I would test the workflow with teams working on real projects and varied task volumes. Important scenarios include changing an assignee, updating task status, reviewing a submission, inviting a member, handling overdue work and recovering from permission or connection errors. Voice and video flows need separate reliability, accessibility and device testing. I would not claim increased productivity without a measured baseline and follow-up.

What I chose not to ship yet

The project and team names, task counts, dates, assignees and activity shown in the screens are illustrative. This screen set represents a project task table and member dashboard; it does not present the current implementation as a working calendar or claim that team productivity has been measured.
