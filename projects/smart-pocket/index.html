Product Design

Smart Pocket

Smart Pocket is a mobile finance concept for people who manage several priorities from one income. Set income and timing, split money into named pockets, then track what is available and spent. Auto-split rules make the plan visible. The Invest tab clearly shows that investing is not open yet and offers learning content while a licensed partner is not connected.

View Figma screens

Problem space

One income has to cover bills, everyday spending, emergencies, and personal goals. Without a clear plan, it can be hard to see what money is already committed and what is still free to use.

Design approach

I mapped the journey from income setup to assigning money to pockets, then made the home screen show total balance, unallocated money, monthly spending, and pocket progress. I kept auto-split visible instead of burying it in settings. For investing, I used a clear unavailable state and learning content rather than presenting unverified returns as live financial information.

Key design decisions

  • Keep total balance and unallocated money near the top to show what has not been assigned.
  • Use named pockets with remaining amounts and progress bars so budget status is easy to scan.
  • Make spending, savings, and locked states filterable on the Pockets screen.
  • Show auto-split status on the home screen so people can tell when allocation rules are active.
  • Keep investing unavailable until a licensed partner is connected, and use the Invest tab for education rather than unverified balances or returns.

Product screens

This is a concept design. Amounts shown are sample interface content, not live financial data. The Invest tab is unavailable until a licensed investment partner is connected.

What I would validate next

I would test whether people understand the difference between total balance, unallocated money, and pocket balances; verify that the displayed amounts reconcile across screens; and run usability tests on income setup and auto-split. I would use those findings to refine the wording, accessibility, error states, and trust cues. User testing and live financial integrations have not been completed yet.

What I chose not to ship yet

I deliberately did not present investing as a live feature. The Figma design says a licensed investment partner is not connected yet, so the Invest screen explains the limitation and offers learning content instead of implying that users can buy funds or earn the sample returns shown in earlier concept screens.

Key features

  • Set monthly income, income type, and expected income timing before building a plan.
  • Organise one balance into named pockets such as rent, emergency, transport, family, giving, fun, and eating out.
  • View remaining pocket amounts and spending progress from the home dashboard.
  • Review spending activity and transaction categories in one place.
  • Set and display auto-split rules for planned pockets.
  • Show a clear unavailable state and financial learning content for investing until a licensed investment partner is connected.
