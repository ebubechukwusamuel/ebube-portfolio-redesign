Product Design

Nuvora

Nuvora is an AI and human healthcare product with separate patient, doctor and admin experiences. The current app includes a patient home, AI consultation, doctor booking, appointments, health records and timeline, with a doctor portal for schedules and earnings. The product aims to make healthcare decisions easier to navigate without presenting AI as a replacement for professional care.

Open Nuvora appView original Nuvora Figma UIView project source

Problem space

When people have a health concern, they may not know what the information means, where to go next, or how to reach the right professional. Nuvora brings guidance, health records and routes to human care into one product journey.

Design approach

I built a connected product experience across patient and doctor tasks. For patients, the home screen prioritises starting a health conversation and quick access to booking, hospital search, lab reading and records. The doctor workspace keeps appointment status and earnings information visible. The interface direction uses a dark clinical palette, restrained green and cyan accents, and clear information grouping.

Key design decisions

  • Keep the patient home focused on the next action instead of showing every health feature at once.
  • Provide direct paths to AI consultation, doctor booking, hospital search, lab reading and health records.
  • Separate the patient portal from the doctor workspace because their tasks and permissions differ.
  • Show doctor earnings as a ledger with payout status, not as a wallet that holds user funds.
  • Use explicit human-care escalation and safety wording rather than suggesting AI replaces clinicians.

Product screens

The Figma link opens the original Nuvora UI supplied for this case study, including patient, doctor and admin screens. Names and figures are illustrative. The interface is not evidence of clinical validation or confirmed live integration status.

What I would validate next

I would validate the language used in AI health guidance with healthcare professionals, test escalation and emergency states, check privacy and consent flows, and test patient onboarding and doctor booking with users. Clinical safety, user outcomes and every live integration need separate verification; the screenshots alone do not establish those results.

What I chose not to ship yet

This case study focuses on navigation and product structure: ask, understand, track, and connect to human care. The AI should support health education and decision-making, not claim to replace professional diagnosis. Doctor earnings are presented as ledger records, not as a stored-value wallet.
