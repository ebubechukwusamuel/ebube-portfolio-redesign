Product Design

Nuvora

Nuvora is an AI-assisted healthcare product designed to help people understand a health concern and choose a next step. The patient experience connects an AI consultation with doctor discovery, booking, appointments, nearby hospitals, first aid, lab results and health history. The interface makes room for human care and clearly frames AI guidance as support rather than a replacement for a healthcare professional.

Problem space

A person who has a health question may not know which action to take, how to find an appropriate professional or where to keep track of care. I approached the experience as a navigation problem: make the next step clear, keep common care tasks close at hand and preserve a visible distinction between AI guidance and care from a qualified professional.

Design approach

The home screen prioritizes Book a Doctor, AI Consultation, My Appointments, First Aid and Nearby Hospitals, while keeping a compact summary of medications, consultations and lab results. The consultation interface uses readable conversation turns with text and listening affordances, then reinforces the boundary with a clear AI guidance disclaimer. The appointments screen separates all, active and completed visits and shows status and payment context at a glance.

Key design decisions

  • Make the next action easy to find on the home screen instead of presenting every health feature with equal priority.
  • Separate AI consultation from doctor booking and appointments so the paths to automated guidance and human care remain clear.
  • Keep an AI guidance disclaimer visible in the conversation interface.
  • Use distinct appointment states and filters to make scheduled, completed, cancelled and pending-payment items easier to scan.
  • Design loading, empty, error and escalation states for health-related flows rather than treating the happy path as the whole experience.

Product screens

The patient home, AI consultation and appointments screens come from the supplied Figma file. AI guidance is presented as supportive information and not as a substitute for professional medical advice.

What I would validate next

I would review symptom language and escalation rules with qualified healthcare professionals, then test the main journeys with patients using accessible language and assistive technology. I would also validate consent and privacy controls, emergency guidance, booking changes and cancellations, failed or delayed AI responses, and the handoff from AI guidance to human care. The screens are not evidence of clinical validation or improved health outcomes.

What I chose not to ship yet

This case study documents the patient-facing interface and intended care-navigation workflows. Sample names, symptoms, conditions, appointment dates and prices are illustrative. Clinical safety, privacy, medical accuracy and live integrations require separate validation; the UI should not be interpreted as diagnosis or treatment.
